01 Documentary profile · CNA

Refugee Chef — Anh Lê

Post Direction · Edit · Sound  |  Series won a Gold World Medal, New York Festivals

The full 23-minute film. A Vietnamese boat refugee builds a restaurant business in Copenhagen. Old family photos carry the past, and slow close-ups of food and water carry the feeling. It is shown in full so you can see how a long runtime is paced, not just how a clip is cut.

◆ Relevant to an art-history channel

Let a still image carry the story. When Anh tells the boat escape, the film does not re-enact it. It holds on archive photos and slow black-and-white water. The pace slows for the past and speeds up for the present. A painting video needs the same call: when to stay on one image, and when to move.

02 Documentary profile · CNA

Refugee Chef — Yia Vang

Post Direction · Edit · Sound  |  Series won a Gold World Medal, New York Festivals

A Hmong chef in Minnesota, and the history behind his food. Black-and-white photos and film reels from the Secret War and the refugee camps sit next to present-day kitchen scenes. The archive gives the story its weight.

◆ Relevant to an art-history channel

The voice is the spine, the image follows it. Yia's words run under the archive stills, and the cut moves to a new image when his words name a new idea. On a painting channel the voiceover leads the same way. Each detail on screen appears when the narrator reaches it, and the cut waits through the quiet moments.

03 Investigative explainer · My own channel

DeadTell TV — The Guilt Trip

Creator · Director · Edit

An episode from my own channel, which takes apart everyday manipulation. A short scene plays out, then replays moment by moment so you can see the one move that gives it away. The actors are AI-generated. AI helps draft the research and scripts, and I rewrite, direct and edit every episode.

◆ Relevant to an art-history channel

Slow down and point at the one detail. The whole format is about naming what a viewer would miss, such as a word, a pause or a look. It is the same job as a close-up on a brushstroke: hold the frame, then tell the viewer what to see. It is also a calm, voice-led format that keeps a viewer watching.

The through-line

Archive stills, slow moves and a voice that leads the cut. Broadcast deadlines taught me to deliver on time, so a steady weekly schedule is familiar work.
